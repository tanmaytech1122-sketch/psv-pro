# API 521 — Pressure-Relieving and Depressuring Systems

## Overview

API Standard 521 covers the causes of overpressure, design principles for pressure-relieving systems, and requirements for flare and disposal systems in petroleum refineries and related industries.

## Overpressure Scenarios

### Blocked Outlet
The most common relief scenario. Occurs when flow is blocked downstream (valve closure, pump cavitation, etc.). The relief flow equals the maximum process flow to the equipment.

Design basis: Maximum feed rate to protected equipment.

### Fire Case (External Fire)
Occurs when fire heats the vessel contents. Applicable primarily to liquid-filled vessels.

**Wetted area formula (API 521 §5.15):**
Aw = exposed wetted surface area (ft²) based on:
- Vessels: wetted area up to 25 ft above grade or average flame height
- For liquid-full vessels: use total wetted surface
- For partially filled vessels: wetted area = liquid-wetted area

**Heat input from fire:**
- With firefighting and drainage: Q = 21,000 × F × Aw^0.82 (BTU/h)
- Without firefighting or drainage: Q = 34,500 × F × Aw^0.82 (BTU/h)
- F = environmental factor (1.0 for bare vessel, 0.3 for insulated)

**Vapour generation rate:** W = Q / λ
Where λ is the latent heat of vaporization (BTU/lb).

**Overpressure allowance for fire:** 21% above set pressure.

### Cooling Water Failure
Loss of cooling water to condensers causes vapour breakthrough. Relief rate = heat duty divided by latent heat.

### Reflux Failure
Loss of reflux to distillation column causes column to flood and overpressure.

### Power Failure
Pumps and compressors trip; blocked outlet may result.

### Heat Exchanger Tube Failure (Tube Rupture)
High-pressure side tube fails into low-pressure shell. Relief rate based on flow through failed tube at maximum differential pressure.

**Flow through failed tube (API 521 §5.19):**
- Critical flow formula for gas-filled tubes
- Uses orifice flow with Cd = 0.61 for clean tube failure
- Relief area must handle two-hole flow (both ends of ruptured tube)

### Runaway Reaction
Exothermic reaction produces excess heat; vapour generation may exceed design capacity. Requires reactive chemistry analysis (DIERS methodology).

### Thermal Expansion (Blocked Liquid)
Liquid in a blocked-in line or equipment expands due to heat. Small but important scenario for liquid-full systems.

**Thermal relief formula:**
Q_BTU = H × (B × q) / (500 × G × Cp)
Where B = coefficient of thermal expansion, q = heat input rate, G = specific gravity, Cp = specific heat.

## Depressurising (Blowdown) Systems

### Purpose
Emergency depressurisation reduces vessel pressure to a safe level (typically 50% of design pressure or 100 psig, whichever is lower) within a specified time (typically 15 minutes to API 521 standard).

### Design Basis
- Target pressure: 50% of design pressure or 100 psig (whichever is lower)
- Time requirement: 15 minutes per API 521
- Consider: auto-refrigeration, minimum design metal temperature (MDMT)

### Blowdown Valve Sizing
The blowdown valve (depressurising valve) Cv is sized to achieve the target depressurisation rate while respecting:
- Minimum temperature limitations (MDMT)
- Maximum flare system capacity
- Safe isothermal or adiabatic assumptions

### ODE Model for Depressurisation
Time-stepping integration:
- dP/dt = -G_mass / (V × ρ) × (k × R × T)
- Temperature changes adiabatically during blowdown
- Gas properties (Z, k) update at each step

## Flare and Disposal Systems

### Flare System Components
- Individual PRV discharge lines
- Collection headers
- Knockout drum (liquid separation)
- Seal drum (liquid seal for flame arrestment)
- Flare stack or enclosed combustion unit

### Header Design
- Pressure drop in flare headers must not cause excessive back pressure on PRVs
- Mach number in headers should not exceed 0.5 (subsonic flow)
- Sonic flow at relief valve outlet is acceptable but headers must be designed for it

### Knockout Drum Sizing
- Vertical or horizontal drum
- Must separate liquid droplets > 300–600 microns
- Sizing based on gas velocity and terminal settling velocity of liquid droplets

## Reaction Forces

When a PRV discharges to atmosphere or into short discharge piping, the reaction force must be considered:

**Open discharge (API 521 Eq. A-10):**
F = W × √(k × R × T / (MW × (k+1))) / gc + A × P1 × (2/(k+1))^(k/(k-1))

Where:
- F = reaction force (lbf)
- W = relief flow rate (lb/s)
- k = ratio of specific heats
- T = relieving temperature (°R)
- MW = molecular weight
- A = orifice area (in²)
- P1 = relieving pressure (psia)

**Dynamic Load Factor (DLF):** Typically 2.0 for instantaneous valve opening. Applied to static reaction force to account for dynamic amplification.

## PSV Installation Considerations

### Inlet Piping
- Pressure drop in inlet piping must not exceed 3% of set pressure
- Excessive inlet loss causes valve chatter (instability)
- Short, direct inlet nozzle preferred

### Outlet Piping
- Back pressure from outlet piping reduces valve capacity
- Must account for built-up back pressure in sizing
- Discharge piping must be adequately supported against reaction forces

### Multiple PRVs (Staggered Set Pressures)
When two or more PRVs protect the same vessel:
- First valve set at design pressure
- Second valve set 5% higher (or per code requirements)
- Supplemental valve may be set up to 10% above MAWP for fire case
