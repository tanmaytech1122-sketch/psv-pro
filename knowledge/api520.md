# API 520 — Sizing, Selection, and Installation of Pressure-Relieving Devices

## Overview

API Standard 520 provides guidance for the sizing, selection, and installation of pressure relief devices used in refineries and related industries. It is divided into two parts:
- **Part I**: Sizing and Selection
- **Part II**: Installation

## Scope

API 520 applies to pressure relief valves (PRVs) and rupture disks installed on pressure vessels and related equipment operating at pressures above 15 psig. It covers gas/vapour, steam, liquid, and two-phase services.

## Key Definitions

- **Set Pressure**: The pressure at which the PRV begins to open, measured at the valve inlet.
- **Relieving Pressure**: The pressure at the valve inlet during relief; equals set pressure × (1 + overpressure fraction).
- **Overpressure**: The pressure increase above set pressure during relief. For non-fire cases, typically 10% or 3 psi (whichever is greater). For fire cases, 21%.
- **Back Pressure**: Pressure at the valve outlet. Affects capacity and valve selection.
- **Blowdown**: Pressure reduction below set pressure before valve reseats. Typically 7–10%.

## Gas and Vapour Sizing (§3.6)

The required orifice area for gas/vapour service is calculated using:

**A = W / (C × Kd × P1 × Kb × Kc) × √(T × Z / MW)**

Where:
- A = required effective discharge area (in²)
- W = required relieving capacity (lb/h)
- C = gas constant based on ratio of specific heats (k)
- Kd = effective coefficient of discharge (0.975 for certified valves)
- P1 = relieving pressure (psia) = set pressure + overpressure + atmospheric
- Kb = back pressure correction factor
- Kc = combination correction factor for rupture disk (1.0 without disk)
- T = relieving temperature (°R = °F + 459.67)
- Z = compressibility factor
- MW = molecular weight

**Critical vs Subcritical Flow:**
Critical (choked) flow occurs when P2/P1 < (2/(k+1))^(k/(k-1)).
For critical flow, the full API 520 §3.6 formula applies.
For subcritical flow, a modified formula with pressure ratio correction is used.

## Steam Sizing (§3.7)

Steam sizing uses the Napier equation:
**A = W / (51.45 × P1 × Kd × Kb × Ksh × Kn × Kc)**

Where:
- Ksh = superheat correction factor (1.0 for saturated steam)
- Kn = Napier steam correction factor (1.0 for pressures below 1500 psia)

## Liquid Sizing (§3.8)

For liquid service:
**A = Q / (38.0 × Kd × Kw × Kc × Kv) × √(G / (P1 - P2))**

Where:
- Q = flow rate (US gal/min)
- G = specific gravity relative to water at 70°F
- Kw = back pressure correction for liquid (1.0 for conventional valves)
- Kv = viscosity correction factor (iterate from Reynolds number)
- P1 = relieving pressure (psia)
- P2 = back pressure (psia)

## Two-Phase Sizing (Appendix C)

Uses the Omega method (Leung, 1990):
1. Calculate omega parameter from flash calculations
2. Determine critical mass flux using omega
3. Calculate required area from relief rate and mass flux

## Correction Factors

### Back Pressure Correction (Kb)
- **Conventional valves**: Kb = 1.0 for back pressure ≤ 10% of set pressure; decreases to ~0.6 at 40%.
- **Balanced-bellows valves**: Kb = 1.0 up to 50% back pressure; decreases to ~0.50 at 80%.
- **Pilot-operated valves**: Kb = 1.0 regardless of back pressure.

### Viscosity Correction (Kv)
Applied only for liquid service. Calculated iteratively from Reynolds number at valve:
Re = 2800 × G × Q / (μ × √A)
Kv is read from API 520 Figure 31 or calculated from Kv = (0.9935Re) / √(Re + 500).

Always select the next standard orifice size larger than the calculated required area.

## Valve Types

### Conventional Spring-Loaded PRV
- Opening is affected by back pressure
- Suitable for back pressure < 10% of set pressure (or up to 30% with Kb correction)
- Most common and economical

### Balanced-Bellows PRV
- Opening is independent of back pressure up to ~50%
- Bellows neutralizes effect of variable back pressure
- Suitable for built-up back pressure up to 50% of set pressure

### Pilot-Operated PRV
- Main valve opened by pilot signal; opening not affected by back pressure
- Suitable for high back pressure (up to 100% of set pressure for some designs)
- Used for tight shutoff and high set-pressure applications
- Can operate at pressures up to 98% of MAWP (vs 90% for spring-loaded)

## Installation Requirements (Part II)

- Inlet pressure drop should not exceed 3% of set pressure during flow
- Discharge piping should be designed to minimize back pressure
- Drain provisions required for valves discharging to atmosphere
- Lifting levers required for most steam and air service
- Valve orientation: springs must be vertical unless specifically designed otherwise
